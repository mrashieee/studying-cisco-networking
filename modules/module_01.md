# Communications in a Connected World

## Network Types

- Home Networks: Computers and Internet.
- SOHO(Small Office/Home Office) Networks: Connect to a corporate
  network and/or access resources.
- Medium to Large Networks: Branches with connected Hundreds or
  Thousands of Hosts(computers).
- World Wide Network: Network of millions of computers world wide.

## Data Transmission

- **What is Data**: Data is a collection of facts, values,
  observations, or information that can be stored, processed, or
  communicated.

### Data Types

- **Volunteer Data**: Data that we intentionally or consciously provide.
- **Inferred Data**: Data that is predicted from other available data.
- **Observed Data**: Data that is collected by observing our
  behaviour, actions or activities.
  
### The Bit

Represents smallest piece of data. The term bit is an abbreviation of
"binary digit". collection of 8 bits are known as a byte.

- **ASCII**: Old computers used American Standard Code for Information
  Interchange, 7-bit binary numbers that represented Numbers,
  Alphabets, and Special Characters. There is also **Extended ASCII**
  which is 8-bit binary numbers.
  
- **UTF-8**: This format is mostly used nowadays in modern systems,
 has variable width (1-4 bytes). Its first 128 are plain ASCII, which
 is why ASCII text works everywhere.

### Common ways of Data Transmission

After data is turned into small bits of binary data, it must be
transmitted across network media to its destination. Media is the
physical medium in which the signals are transmitted. Copper wire,
fiber-optic cable, and electromagnetic waves are used as medium to
transmit signals. The medium produces electrical or optical patterns
that are transmitted between devices. Devices along the way read the
signal and rebuild it for the next device until it reaches its
destination.

## Bandwidth

Bandwidth is the capacity of a medium to carry data. It measures the
amount of data that can flow from one place to another in a given
amount of time. It is measured in amount of data/bits sent in a
second.

Commonly used units of measure for bandwidth.

| Unit of Bandwidth   | Abbreviation | Equivalence                                          |
|:--------------------|:-------------|:-----------------------------------------------------|
| Bits per second     | bps          | 1 bps = fundamental unit of bandwidth                |
| Kilobits per second | Kbps         | 1 Kbps = 1,000 bps = 10<sup>3</sup> bps              |
| Megabits per second | Mbps         | 1 Mbps = 1,000,000 bps = 10<sup>6</sup> bps          |
| Gigabits per second | Gbps         | 1 Gbps = 1,000,000,000 bps = 10<sup>9</sup> bps      |
| Terabits per second | Tbps         | 1 Tbps = 1,000,000,000,000 bps = 10<sup>12</sup> bps |

## Throughput

It is the actual amount of data that travels through per second, which
can be equal or less than bandwidth. It depends on amount and type of
data, also Latency created by the number of network devices
encountered between source and destination.

When you use a speedtester on your devices, it shows throughput, not
bandwidth.

In a big network with multiple segments, throughput will always depend
on slowest link, even if most of the segments have high bandwidth, it
will take the lowest bandwidth device to create a slowdown of the
entire network.

### Latency
It is the time (including delays) of data to travel from one place to
another. Latency is measured in milliseconds (ms).
