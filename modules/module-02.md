# Network Components, Types, and Connections

## Clients and Servers

Any device on a network is a host. Hosts can send and receive messages
on a network. Nowadays, a computer host can act as a client, a server,
or both depending on the software it is using.

### Server

Servers are hosts that have software which can provide information and
services to other hosts on the network.

### Client

Clients are hosts that have software which enables them to get
information and services from the servers on the network.

### Peer-to-Peer (P2P) Networks

When hosts run both client and server software at the same time, the
resulting network is called a peer-to-peer (P2P) network.

Simple example for this is file sharing between two devices, they can
exchange data with each other, acting as either client or server as
necessary.

#### Merits of P2P

- Easy to set up.
- Less complex.
- Lower cost because network devices and dedicated servers may not be
  required.
- Can be used for simple tasks such as transferring files and sharing
  printers.

#### Demerits of P2P

- No centralized administration.
- Not as secure.
- Not scalable.
- All devices may act as both clients and servers which can slow their
  performance.

## Network Components

- **End Devices**: Devices at network's edge that users interact with.
- **Intermediate Devices**: Devices that move packets between end
  devices.
- **Network Media**: Wireless or physical cabling used to connect end
  devices to each other and to intermediate devices.

### End Devices

These are the devices which are either the source or destination of
data transmitted over the network. It is sent and received by uniquely
identifying hosts using their addresses.

## ISP (Internet Service Provider) Services

Internet Service Provider provides the connection between the home
network and Internet. ISP can be the local cable provider, a landline
telephone service provider, the cellular network that provides your
smartphone service, or an independent provider who leases bandwidth on
the physical network infrastructure of another company. The primary
medium that connects the Internet backbone which goes underground and
undersea to connect various continents, countries, and cities is
fiber-optic cable.

### ISP Connections

The backbone of the internet is a complex web of fiber-optic cables
with expensive networking switches and routers that direct the flow of
information between source and destination hosts. Average users never
see this infrastructure, they only ever talk to their own ISP. At home
there are two ways online: a modem straight to the PC, which leaves
the computer unprotected, or a router in between, which is the safe
normal setup. The router includes a switch to connect wired hosts and
a wireless AP (Access Point) for wireless devices.


### Cable and DSL Connections

- **Cable**: Offered by cable television providers, Internet rides on
  the same coaxial cable as TV. The modem splits the internet signal
  out and gives you an Ethernet cable to plug into your PC or router.
  
- **DSL (Digital Subscriber Line)**: Offered by phone companies,
  internet runs over the telephone line. There are three ways: voice
  calls (telephone), download, and upload. Speed depends on line
  quality and distance from the phone company's office.

### Other Connectivity Options

- **Cellular**: Internet over the mobile network, used by devices with
  a SIM. Speed depends on signal and congestion, also it is usually
  metered, so if you cross your data limit then you will have to pay
  extra.

- **Satellite**: Offered by satellite providers, for homes that don't
  have access to cable or DSL. It needs satellite dishes which require
  clear sky view, so not possible to setup in wooded areas or with
  other overhead obstructions. Speed will vary depending on the
  contract/plan. Installation costs can be high.

- **Dial-up Telephone**: Modem phones the ISP over the regular voice
  line. Ties up the line while you are online and tops out around 56
  Kbps. Only used where other options don't exist.
