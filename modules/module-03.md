# Wireless and Mobile Networks

## Wireless Networks

- **GSM**: the old 2G system, built for calls and texts. Your SIM
  proves who you are to the tower. Data is slow, voice was the
  point.

- **4G (LTE)**: built for data instead of voice. Wider channels,
  real internet speeds. Most phones live here most of the time.

- **5G**: wider channels again plus smarter antennas, so faster
  peaks and lower delay. Needs denser towers, which is why
  coverage is still patchy.

- **GPS**: Uses satellites around Earth to transmit signals. The
  smartphone receives these signals and predicts the phone's location
  within 10 meters.

- **Wi-Fi**: When a phone is within the range of the signal from a
  wireless network access point it can connect to that Wi-Fi. This
  will connect phone to local network and the internet. Hotspot is an
  area where Wi-Fi signals are available. Smartphones can create
  signals with functionality also called hotspot.

- **Bluetooth**: Used for accessories such as speakers, headphones,
  microphones and many smart appliances. It is a low-power, shorter
  range wireless technology intended to replace wired
  connectivity. Multiple devices can be connected at the same time
  with Bluetooth creating a small network. It can be used for
  transmitting data.

- **NFC (Near Field Communication)**: It is a wireless communication
  technology used to enable transferring data in very close proximity,
  usually less than a few centimeters. Mostly used in payment systems.

## Mobile Device Connectivity

Most of this is stuff I already know from daily use - Wi-Fi and
cellular data are not hard, you just do them.

- **Wi-Fi**: connect with the SSID (Service Set Identifier) and
  passphrase. Use WPA2 or higher encryption for security.
- **Cellular data**: takes more battery than Wi-Fi.
- **Bluetooth**: various devices like speakers and headphones, and
  how to connect to them. Pairing means putting the device in
  discoverable mode and then scanning with the other device.
  During pairing, a personal identification number (PIN) may be
  requested to authenticate. Once done, next time it pairs
  automatically, no need to redo it.

## Assessment Questions

1. What term is used to describe connecting a mobile device to another
   mobile device or computer to share a network connection?  
   Ans: Tethering

2. Which wireless technology uses a device-pairing process to
   communicate and operates over short distances of 100 meters?  
   Ans: Bluetooth

3. What technology enables a cell phone to be used as a hands-free
   device?  
   Ans: Bluetooth

4. A student purchased a new Wi-Fi-enabled tablet computer. What is
   required to connect this device to the internet?  
   Ans: A wireless LAN

5. Which technology allows a mobile device to establish wireless
   communication with another mobile device by touching them together?  
   Ans: NFC

6. Which technology will allow a mobile device to share an internet
   connection with other devices via tethering?  
   Ans: Bluetooth

7. What are two methods typically used on a mobile device to provide
   internet connectivity?  
   Ans: Wi-Fi and Cellular

8. Which wireless technology allows a customer to connect to a payment
   system with a smartphone?  
   Ans: NFC

9. A salesperson is using a smartphone map application to locate a
   business. What wireless technology enables the smartphone to
   receive satellite geolocation information for the map application?  
   Ans: GPS

10. What two pieces of information are required when manually
    connecting to a secured wireless network?  
	Ans: SSID and Password

11. Which wireless technology can be used to connect wireless
    headphones to a computer?  
	Ans: Bluetooth
